Volume, surface area, centre of mass and mass are measured over the whole subject. A STEP subject is measured from its exact BRep and a mesh from its triangles; the claim is the same either way, and the result names the source it used.

## Claim the properties

The plate is 40 × 40 × 20 mm with a 10 mm bore: 32,000 mm³ less the bore's 1,570.8 mm³. Each `value` is compared within `tolerance`, 0.02 by default, and `density` turns the volume into a mass.

Four claims pass and one is refused: `toHaveMass` without `density` has nothing to measure. Set `volume` to 32000 and it fails by the bore's volume. Switch the model to the gear and every number fails, the evidence `source` changes from `brep` to `mesh`, and the refusal stays a refusal.

## Read the evidence

A passed or failed claim reports the measured value, the expectation the engine applied and the source. The mass claim on the plate measures as:

```json
{
  "measured": 82.15884991765361,
  "normalizedExpected": { "density": 0.0027, "tolerance": 0.02, "value": 82.16 },
  "witnesses": { "density": 0.0027, "source": "brep", "tolerance": 0.02, "volume": 30429.20367320504 }
}
```

A failed claim carries `GEOSPEC_MEASUREMENT_MISMATCH` and says what it measured: `Mass is 82.15884991765361 (brep evidence), which does not satisfy 82 within 0.02.`

## Mass needs a density

No admitted subject carries a material, so `toHaveMass` refuses with `GEOSPEC_EVIDENCE_UNSUPPORTED` unless the payload declares `density`. The mass unit follows the density: grams per mm³ gives grams, so the plate in aluminium at 0.0027 g/mm³ weighs 82.16 g. The Python facade raises on the refusal and returns a report on the pass:

```python title="test_plate.py"
from geospec import GeoSpecAssertionError, GeoSpecEngine, expect_geo, load_model

with GeoSpecEngine(model_root=".") as engine:
    plate = load_model(file="plate-with-through-bore.step", format="step")
    try:
        expect_geo(plate).to_have_mass(value=82.16)
    except GeoSpecAssertionError as error:
        print(error.code, dict(error.result)["status"])
    report = expect_geo(plate).to_have_mass(value=82.16, density=0.0027)
    print(report.passed, report.result["status"], report.result["evidence"]["measured"])
```

```console
GEOSPEC_EVIDENCE_UNSUPPORTED refused
True passed 82.15884991765361
```

## BRep versus mesh

The gear measures 911.53 mm³ from its 624 triangles, and its surface area, 910.80 mm², is the sum of the triangle areas. A mesh approximates curved faces, so its area and volume differ from the BRep of the same part; claim the numbers of the export you admitted. An open mesh still yields a volume and a centroid, so pair the claims with `toBeWatertight` from [Check meshes](/docs/guides/check-meshes).

## Variations

**A range instead of a value.** `value: { greaterThan: 30000, lessThan: 31000 }` passes on the plate. The comparisons are strict as written; tolerance applies to `value` only. [Set tolerances and budgets](/docs/guides/set-tolerances-and-budgets) has the rules.

**Centre of mass on chosen axes.** `point: { z: 10 }` constrains z and leaves x and y free. A failed claim lists each axis that missed under `failures`, with the actual and expected values.

**A nominal volume.** `tolerance: 0` on `value: 30429.2` fails, because the plate measures 30429.20367 mm³. State the value you computed and a tolerance that covers your rounding, not the engine's.

**A negated claim.** With `polarity: 'negative'`, a claim the geometry satisfies fails with `GEOSPEC_NEGATED_MATCH`. The mass claim without `density` is refused under either polarity.

**The reference.** The [measurement reference](/docs/reference/measurement#tohavemass) lists every payload field and the admitted geometry of each claim; [Check dimensions](/docs/guides/check-dimensions) covers the bounds the same evidence yields.
